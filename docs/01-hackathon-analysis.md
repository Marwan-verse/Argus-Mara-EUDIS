## 1.2 The three challenges (verbatim from the Irish site)

**1. Navigation without GNSS**
> Autonomous systems that can navigate and operate effectively without GNSS, using onboard sensors and real-time perception. Develop or use data from quantum sensors (e.g. accelerometers, gravimeters and quantum imaging), which offer advantages such as navigation without GPS, the detection of underground structures, and enabling non-line-of-sight perception.

**2. Coordination of Autonomous Swarms**
> Develop a multi-agent drone or robot swarm that can collaborate to complete a mission (e.g. search and rescue, mapping or target tracking) with minimal human control and include optimisation using quantum simulation.

**3. Department of Defence Challenge: Space-Enabled Maritime Domain Awareness**
> Develop an autonomous or AI-enabled capability that uses data from space and/or remote sensors to detect, monitor, interpret or predict activity in real time within the maritime environment.

Solutions may focus on:
- protection of critical undersea infrastructure
- detection of suspicious maritime behaviour
- autonomous surveillance of large ocean areas
- space-enabled ISR
- AI-assisted interpretation of satellite imagery
- maritime anomaly detection
- predictive risk modelling
- multi-domain data fusion
- decision support for commanders and operators

**Other hosts' third challenges show the pattern:**
- Cyprus: counter-drone interceptor for critical infrastructure.
- Latvia: an "Autonomous Border Shield" for low-altitude threats.
- Sweden: low-cost early warning of low-altitude drones.
- Austria: soldier protection.
- Portugal: motherships and hybrid swarms.

Ireland is the only host that chose **maritime and space**.

## 1.4 How it is judged

- **Irish rubric: not published. Ask the organisers.** EUDIS guidance for local rounds is **technological maturity, operational relevance and market potential**. The Autumn 2025 winners were praised for "creativity, technical excellence, and potential to contribute to Europe's defence and space capabilities".
- **EU-wide final:** progress, relevance, team, innovativeness. **Progress** is measured over the roughly 2-month mentoring period, so the pitch needs a visible "next 8 weeks" plan.
- The organisers describe the programme as wanting "**not concepts but working prototypes that can withstand scrutiny from people who know the field**".

| Mar 2026 | Defending airspace | Scaled Autonomy (NL, 2nd, **live flight** of a GPS-free autonomous FPV interceptor); SkyHunter (RO, its second win); Tiepoint (NO) |

**Patterns in the winners:**
1. **Mature teams with an existing core.** They used the 48 hours to *adapt* something that already worked.
2. **A working demo.** Hardware if possible, and a number against a baseline every time. For example, an EDTH winner showed "23% faster time-to-detect than a lawnmower search at equal path length".
3. **A cost-per-effect or cost-of-failure argument.**
4. **A named user and real data.** AEREUS used real Ukrainian recon footage; Tiepoint works with Ukrainian units.
5. **Resilient by design:** mesh networks, distributed systems, degraded-mode operation.
6. **A visible next step:** an accelerator, an EDF consortium or a test campaign.

**Common ways to lose:**
- slides with no working demo, or a demo that doesn't run end to end;
- building the whole system but proving no single layer of it;
- no baseline;
- dashboards on synthetic data only;
- unrealistic "GNSS denial" with no drift and no spoofing;
- quantum used as decoration;
- no customer and no next step.

## 1.6 The Irish context in numbers

**Maritime**
- Ireland's EEZ is about **7× its land area**, reaching about 370 km into the Atlantic.
- The Naval Service has **8 ships but crews for about 2 at sea at a time** (Irish Times, May 2026).
- About **11 North America–Europe fibre cables** pass through or near Irish waters, a transatlantic "choke point".
- Roughly 75–80% of Ireland's gas arrives by subsea pipelines from Great Britain †. Power interconnectors include Greenlink, EWIC and the Celtic Interconnector (due about 2027–28) †.
- **Russian activity:**
  - The *Yantar* loitered near cables and a gas pipeline in Nov 2024 while flying 3 drones.
  - In Aug 2025, the sanctioned, falsely flagged tanker *Blue* (4 identities and 6 flags since 2020) **switched off its AIS before entering Ireland's EEZ**.
  - Since late Mar 2026, **shadow-fleet tankers have rerouted west of Ireland**, including a convoy of 5 EU-sanctioned tankers in Apr 2026. They are the same class of ageing, sanctioned ships linked to Baltic cable cuts. *Eagle S* (Dec 2024) slowed from 12 to 9 kn and left a drag line of about 90 km.
  - Legislation to give the Naval Service **boarding powers** is being drafted.
- **Policy direction:**
  - The National Maritime Security Strategy (Feb 2026) plans a **national maritime security centre** joining civil and military information for faster decisions. It names **space-based monitoring** as an area to develop.
  - EU defence ministers met in **Wicklow (31 Aug–1 Sep 2026)**. Their agenda included space in security and defence, and maritime security.
- **Existing Irish projects:**
  - **Sea-Scan** (TCD/ADAPT/CONNECT) won Research Ireland's Defence Innovation Challenge, with about €1.2m.
    - It turns subsea telecom fibre into a sensor (distributed acoustic sensing plus polarisation sensing) that detects dark vessels, anchor drops and trawling near cables.
    - It is trained with weak supervision from imperfect AIS labels, and reports 97.8% detection at a 1.98% false-trigger rate.
    - Its field study used the Dublin–North Wales link (Emerald Bridge Fibres).
  - **GUARD** (Tyndall, €7.6m from the Disruptive Technologies Innovation Fund, DTIF) builds long-endurance drones, up to about 17 h, launched and recovered from Naval Service ships.
- The DF describes using **AI to cover shortfalls in resources and personnel**.

**Air and drones**
- On **1 Dec 2025**, during President Zelensky's arrival, 4–5 "military-style" drones flew in a no-fly zone off Howth. They **orbited LÉ William Butler Yeats for about 2 hours**. The ship could only film them, and the operator is still unidentified.
- Since then:
  - a €19m counter-drone (jamming) system;
  - MBDA Sky Warden with net-firing "Drone Hunter" interceptors;
  - offshore counter-drone is still "under procurement";
  - 5 Thales long-range radars ordered, delivered from 2027, full air picture by end-2028.

**Policy**
- Ireland holds the EU Council Presidency from Jul to Dec 2026.
- Ireland consistently supports **international regulation of autonomous weapons** under *meaningful human control* †. The CCW Review Conference is in Nov 2026, a month after the hackathon †.
- The Irish Times reported (May 2026) that the new Scorpion armoured vehicles have AI "slew-to-cue". Autonomy near weapons is a sensitive topic in Ireland.
- **Budget 2027 is published in early October**. Check the defence figure the week before the event.

**Money right after the event**
- **Research Ireland Defence Innovation Challenge 2026 closes 29 Oct 2026**, 12 days after the hackathon.
- It offers up to €220k over 13 months, plus a €1m prize phase.
- Area 1 is *space-based situational awareness and security*.
- **But:** the intent-to-apply deadline was **15 Sep 2026**, and applicants must be eligible Irish research bodies. It is only reachable through a partner who already registered.

## 1.7 Strategic insights

1. **This is a maritime hackathon wearing a battlefield badge.** The Department of Defence chose maritime and space when every other host chose drones. The Irish problems the jury lives with are the empty sea, the cables, the shadow fleet and the crew shortage. Anchor any idea in them, whichever challenge you pick.
2. **The customer wrote Challenge 3.** A strong C3 entry is judged by the people who will buy it. Map your solution explicitly to the National Maritime Security Strategy objectives.
3. **Maturity beats novelty, and you're allowed to start now.** Pre-existing work was *favoured* at selection, as long as it is declared. You have three weeks. Arrive with a working skeleton and spend the 2.5 days making it impressive.
4. **Show a number against a baseline.** For example: "Our alert fires 94 minutes before the cable crossing; the naive geofence fires 11× more false alarms."
5. **Quantum: honest or not at all.**
   - Challenge 2 *requires* "optimisation using quantum simulation"; in Challenge 1 it is encouraged.
   - A Dublin audience includes the Irish quantum community (Tyndall, Equal1, ICHEC †), and they will probe it.
   - Formulate the problem properly (for example as a QUBO, a problem format that quantum and quantum-inspired solvers take) and benchmark it against a classical method.
   - Say where quantum does *not* help yet.
6. **Autonomy that protects; humans who decide.**
   - Frame autonomy as navigation, sensing, coordination and decision *support*: search and rescue, surveillance, infrastructure protection.
   - Put a human authorisation gate in front of anything consequential.
   - Talk of "autonomous targeting" will cost you with an Irish Department of Defence jury.
7. **Don't rebuild what Ireland already funded; connect to it.** Sea-Scan (fibre sensing on cables) and GUARD (ship-launched drones) are DF-backed. A pitch that says "we plug into them" shows you know the ecosystem. A pitch that duplicates them invites an awkward comparison.
8. **Design for the follow-on:**
   - the Defence Innovation Challenge (29 Oct), but only through a partner who registered intent by 15 Sep;
   - ESA BIC, which needs a space link;
   - DF RTI trials;
   - TensorX credits;
   - EUDIS mentoring, followed by an EU-wide final judged on *progress*.
   - The best idea is one that every sponsor in the room can say yes to.
9. **Prize spread.** There is one podium across three challenges. Many juries like to reward different challenges, though that is not guaranteed. So being clearly the best team *in your challenge* matters.

## 1.8 Likely competition

- **Challenge 3** will probably draw the most teams: it is software-only, has lots of open satellite and AIS data, and attracts the space/AI crowd.
  - Expect several "dark-vessel detector" and "AIS anomaly dashboard" entries.
  - Win it by being **infrastructure-specific, predictive and decision-oriented**, not by building another detector.
  - Commercial AIS "cable watch" services already exist (NKT, Sentinel Proactive, UltramapGlobal, Gatehouse Maritime, OSI). Expect judges to ask how you differ. See [06-challenge-3-deep-dive.md §6.7](06-challenge-3-deep-dive.md).
- **Challenges 1 and 2** will draw drone and robotics teams, including teams with Ukrainian battlefield experience.
  - Expect strong visual-navigation entries.
  - The quantum requirement in C2 will put some teams off, which could leave a well-executed C2 entry with little competition.
- Selection favoured MVPs, so expect a few **registered startups** with real prototypes.
