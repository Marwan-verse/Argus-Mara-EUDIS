# Decisions log

This is the team's record of decisions. Add new ones at the bottom, and never silently rewrite old ones: if something changes, add a new entry that supersedes it.

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | 24 Sep 2026 | **We compete in Challenge 3**, the Department of Defence's "Space-Enabled Maritime Domain Awareness" challenge. | The customer wrote it. It matches Ireland's real maritime gaps, and it is buildable as software in the time we have. |
| D2 | 24 Sep 2026 | **The product is called ARGUS MARA.** | *Argus Panoptes* is the hundred-eyed watchman of Greek myth who never slept all at once. *Mara* is Irish for "of the sea" (as in *Foras na Mara*, the Marine Institute). The bare name "Argus" is heavily used (ARGUS-IS, Argus Media, CLS Argos, an earlier hackathon project), so *Mara* makes it distinctive and searchable. |
| D3 | 24 Sep 2026 | **The countdown timer is the heart of the product.** Every flagged ship gets a timer to the cable or pipeline it threatens, and every response option shows whether it arrives in time. The core function is called **Anchor Watch**. | Other teams will show "ships on a map". A countdown answers the commander's real question: how much time do we have, and what can we do in it? |
| D4 | 24 Sep 2026 | **Complement Trinity's Sea-Scan; don't compete with it.** Sea-Scan listens at the cable (fibre sensing). Argus Mara sees the ship hours earlier from AIS and space, knows who it is, and plans the response. We propose a two-way cue plus better training labels for them, and a joint pilot. | Research Ireland and the Defence Forces funded Sea-Scan, and the jury knows it. Showing the ecosystem joined up is stronger than showing a rival. |
| D5 | 24 Sep 2026 | **The three "special" features are fixed:** (1) the countdown with arrival-in-time checks; (2) anchor-reach physics, meaning we watch only seabed an anchor can reach; (3) an identity check from space, comparing radar-measured length with AIS-declared length plus flag and name history. Proof comes from replaying the *Eagle S* profile. | These are the points judges should remember. |
| D6 | 24 Sep 2026 | **Principles:** a human decides every action, and nothing is kinetic. Public data only. Fictional vessel names in scenarios. Everything is labelled *real*, *reconstructed* or *simulated*. | This fits Ireland's stance on meaningful human control, and it keeps the demo honest and legally safe. |
| D7 | 24 Sep 2026 | **The team is organised by workstream, not by skill:** Data, Brain, Space, Interface, plus Story (the lead). | The team is versatile, and workstreams let everyone build in parallel against agreed data contracts. |
| D8 | 24 Sep 2026 | **All pre-event work is declared.** Keep `PRIOR_WORK.md`, and run `git tag pre-hackathon` on 14 Oct. | The organisers require us to state clearly what existed before the event. |
| D9 | 27 Sep 2026 | **Team and name origin.** We are our school's **Cyber team**, captained by **Mykhailo**; Mark coordinates both school teams. Mark suggested the name *Argus*. Mykhailo found it was already widely used and added *Mara*. | Keeps a record of roles and credits. |
| D10 | 27 Sep 2026 | **Record Irish AIS traffic 24/7 from now on** with `recorder/` (via aisstream.io). Store both the parsed columns and each raw message as Parquet under `data/ais/`, which is git-ignored. | Our own weeks of Irish traffic are needed to learn "normal" behaviour and to measure false alarms, the headline numbers. |
| D11 | 7 Oct 2026 | **The plan is reset from today.** Nothing from the 24 Sep – 6 Oct phases was built, so those phases are superseded rather than caught up. Eight days remain. The new plan runs day by day from 7 Oct and is in [docs/04-work-plan.md](docs/04-work-plan.md), with the daily Russian version in [docs/plan-ru.md](docs/plan-ru.md). | Pretending the old schedule still applies would have us start week 2 of a plan whose week 1 never happened. Better to admit it and re-cut the scope to what eight days can hold. |
| D12 | 7 Oct 2026 | **Demo-first: an end-to-end demo by Fri 9 Oct, 20:00**, on invented data. From then on we replace one invented part per day with a real one, and the demo is never allowed to be broken for more than about an hour. | Four perfect components assembled at the end is how teams arrive with nothing that switches on. A complete ugly demo on day 3 makes losing impossible and makes every later day optional. |
| D13 | 7 Oct 2026 | **Roles are fixed, two people per workstream:** Story and integration — Mykhailo (captain); Data — Ihor Maslovskyi and Bohdan; Brain — Nazar Kyrychenko and Myroslav; Space — Nazariy Buryak; Interface — Adam Pohořilský and Marwan. | Pairs talk, and talking unsticks people; a lone owner who gets stuck goes quiet for two days. Space is a single owner because nothing else depends on it, and Nazariy asked for it. Adam and Marwan are paired on Interface, which carries the most visible work. |
| D14 | 7 Oct 2026 | **The v1 demo is file-based and has no backend:** `demo/scenario.json` (*what happened*, written by Data) and `demo/alerts.json` (*what Argus Mara noticed*, written by Brain). The interface reads both and plays them. A live API comes only after the demo already runs. | It lets all five workstreams work in parallel from hour one against a frozen contract, keeps Python and JavaScript people out of each other's way, and gives us a demo that needs no deployment and runs with the Wi-Fi off. |
| D15 | 7 Oct 2026 | **Cut list, agreed in advance:** no learned/ML detector (rules only), no LLM brief, no drones, no new satellite tasking, no real Sea-Scan integration (simulated feed, clearly labelled), no database, logins, mobile layout or design system. | Deciding this now stops the argument recurring every day of a week we cannot spare. Rules also explain better to judges than a model does. |
| D16 | 7 Oct 2026 | **We will have 8 days of our own AIS recording, not three weeks**, because the recorder starts on 7 Oct. We say "eight days" on the slide and never round it up. | The recorder was not started in September. Eight days is still enough to measure false alarms per day, and an accurate small number survives questioning where an inflated one does not. |
